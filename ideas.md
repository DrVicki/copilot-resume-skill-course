## Direction 1

**Very Brief Intro:** A premium editorial course that feels like an investigative career workbook: annotated evidence, precise matching, and decisive action. It makes AI feel accountable and practical rather than magical.

**Probability:** 0.031

## Direction 2

**Theme Name:** Signal Workshop

**Very Brief Intro:** A crisp industrial interface inspired by tool benches, instruction plates, and calibration marks. It frames the learner as someone assembling a dependable professional system.

**Probability:** 0.006

## Direction 3

**Very Brief Intro:** A calm cartographic learning experience with routes, legends, and waypoints. It presents career evidence as a landscape that Copilot helps the learner navigate—not invent.

## Chosen Direction: The Evidence Desk

### Design Movement

The design combines **International Typographic Style** discipline with the tactile intelligence of contemporary independent magazines and annotated research notebooks.

### Core Principles

1. **Evidence before polish:** Claims, examples, and interactions always reinforce the difference between verified career facts and invented résumé language.
2. **Editorial asymmetry:** Strong left alignment, offset columns, oversized numbers, and marginal notes replace generic centered-card composition.
3. **Visible structure:** Rules, connectors, registration marks, labels, and section indices reveal how the system works.
4. **Practical momentum:** Every lesson ends in an action assignment that creates part of the learner’s reusable Copilot setup.

### Color Philosophy

The base is warm archival paper rather than clinical white, giving the course a thoughtful, human quality. Deep blue-black ink creates authority and reading comfort. A vivid **Proof Lime** acts as the ownable signal for verified evidence and successful completion, while restrained cobalt denotes Copilot-assisted actions. The color system avoids decorative gradients and uses contrast to distinguish truth, process, and output.

### Layout Paradigm

The page behaves like an editorial dossier. A slim vertical course rail establishes position; lesson content moves through offset columns, wide evidence tables, numbered interludes, and full-bleed instruction panels. The hero is a two-part composition: a compact course promise on the left and an abstract evidence-to-output visual on the right. On small screens, the rail becomes a compact progress header and the offset structure resolves into a readable single column.

### Signature Elements

1. **Proof marks:** Lime underlines, check stamps, corner ticks, and small “verified” labels mark trustworthy evidence.
2. **Red thread connectors:** Fine cobalt lines trace the route from career evidence to posting requirement to résumé bullet.
3. **Field notes:** Small monospace annotations sit in margins and beside examples, giving the course a coached-workbook feel.

### Interaction Philosophy

Interactions should feel like handling a good research tool: immediate, legible, and reversible. Lesson navigation uses clear states rather than theatrical transitions. Checklists visibly convert into progress, copy buttons confirm action in place, and reveal panels let learners inspect examples without losing their reading position.

### Animation

Entry motion is reserved for the hero diagram and first appearance of major lesson blocks. Elements fade and shift by no more than 12–18 px with a 180–260 ms snappy ease-out. Connector lines draw once as the hero enters. Hover states use tiny directional shifts and ink-to-cobalt color changes. Progress updates use a fast scale-and-color confirmation. All nonessential motion is disabled when reduced motion is preferred.

### Typography System

**Newsreader** is the display face for the course title and key editorial statements, using a mix of roman and italic to create tension between evidence and ambition. **Manrope** handles body copy and interface labels for clarity. **IBM Plex Mono** is reserved for field notes, prompts, indices, and output examples. The title is large but left aligned; section headings rely on contrast in weight and spacing instead of uniform card headers.

### Brand Essence

**Positioning:** A practical résumé systems course for serious job seekers who want Copilot to work from evidence, not improvisation.

**Personality:** Exacting, encouraging, candid.

### Brand Voice

Headlines are short, confident, and slightly provocative. Calls to action describe a concrete action rather than promise transformation. Microcopy is candid about uncertainty and never implies that AI can replace judgment.

**Example headline:** “Stop rewriting. Start building the system.”

**Example CTA:** “Build your source of truth.”

### Wordmark & Logo

The brand is **Proofwork**: a custom narrow wordmark paired with a bold symbol made from three offset document sheets that form a subtle forward arrow and the negative-space letter P. The symbol must remain recognizable at favicon size, use no text, and sit on a transparent background.

### Signature Brand Color

**Proof Lime — `#C8F84A`**. It appears only where evidence is verified, progress is earned, or a decisive action is available, making it semantically meaningful and immediately recognizable.

## Implementation Commitment

Every component will be judged against one question: **Does this choice reinforce the course as an evidence-led editorial tool, or dilute it into a generic AI landing page?**

# Companion Brainstorm: Copilot Interview Practice Lab

## Companion Direction 1

**Theme Name:** The Proofroom

**Very Brief Intro:** An editorial interview studio organized around one illuminated question, one evidence-backed answer, and a visible debrief loop. It feels focused and quietly theatrical without turning practice into performance theater.

**Probability:** 0.008

## Companion Direction 2

**Theme Name:** Signal Booth

**Very Brief Intro:** A mid-century broadcast rehearsal room with cue cards, timing marks, transcript strips, and restrained audio-console geometry. It treats clear interviewing as a signal-quality problem.

**Probability:** 0.064

## Companion Direction 3

**Theme Name:** Counsel Table

**Very Brief Intro:** A rigorous deposition-inspired workspace built from indexed exhibits, question tabs, and exact transcript annotations. It makes follow-up questions feel forensic, but risks becoming too severe for an encouraging course.

**Probability:** 0.023

## Chosen Companion Direction: The Proofroom

### Design Movement

The Proofroom combines **1960s editorial portraiture**, **International Typographic Style**, and the physical logic of a carefully lit interview set. It keeps the parent brand’s evidence discipline while shifting from document archive to rehearsal room.

### Core Principles

1. **One question in focus:** The composition always establishes what the learner is answering now and what remains outside the frame.
2. **Evidence under the performance:** Transcript lines, proof-story cards, and source ticks remain visible beneath every polished response.
3. **Silence has structure:** Generous dark space and deliberate pauses make the course feel calm rather than crowded.
4. **Debrief, then repeat:** Every visual sequence returns from live practice to evidence review and a smaller next-round target.

### Color Philosophy

The parent brand’s warm paper and deep blue-black remain the foundation. A new **Room Red** introduces the energy of an illuminated recording lamp and marks live-round states, while Proof Lime remains reserved for verified evidence and completed practice. Cobalt identifies Copilot-assisted analysis. Red and lime never become decorative gradients; they distinguish live pressure from earned proof.

### Layout Paradigm

The page behaves like a rehearsal studio seen in section. A wide interview stage carries the current lesson while a narrow transcript gutter records question order, evidence checks, and completion state. Large question marks, chair-like vertical silhouettes, time codes, and offset scorecards replace the resume course’s document-stack emphasis. Mobile layouts collapse into a single transcript flow with the active question and progress controls kept adjacent.

### Signature Elements

1. **Question aperture:** A large outlined question mark or spotlight circle frames the active learning problem.
2. **Transcript gutter:** Monospaced time-code labels and short evidence checks create a persistent interview record.
3. **Room lamp:** A precise Room Red signal marks live practice; it turns Proof Lime only after the learner completes the evidence debrief.

### Interaction Philosophy

Controls should feel like beginning, pausing, or reviewing a practice round. Lesson selection resembles choosing a segment from a transcript. Completion checks use an explicit two-state signal rather than celebratory animation. Copy and download actions confirm immediately and remain reversible.

### Animation

Use a 220 ms aperture reveal for major lesson headers, a 140 ms Room Red signal change for live states, and 30–50 ms staggered transcript-line entries. Avoid pulsing or looping recording lights. Freeze all nonessential motion when reduced motion is preferred.

### Typography System

Retain **Newsreader** for editorial authority, **Manrope** for readable instruction, and **IBM Plex Mono** for time codes, prompt text, source notes, and transcript labels. Companion headlines use tighter line spacing and more upright roman serif than the résumé course, reserving italics for reflective questions and the candidate’s internal reasoning.

### Brand Essence

**Positioning:** A repeatable interview-practice studio for job seekers who want Copilot to challenge their evidence without writing the performance for them.

**Personality:** Composed, probing, humane.

### Brand Voice

Headlines sound like a perceptive coach setting one clear condition. Calls to action name a rehearsal behavior rather than promise confidence.

**Example headline:** “Practice the room. Keep the evidence.”

**Example CTA:** “Run one honest round.”

### Wordmark & Logo

The Proofwork parent mark remains in global navigation. The companion uses a secondary **Proofroom aperture**: two offset chair-like brackets forming a question mark in negative space, paired with a single round Room Red signal. The symbol contains no text and remains recognizable at favicon and lesson-tab size.

### Signature Companion Color

**Room Red — `#F04E3E`**. It appears only for live-practice cues, unresolved evidence checks, and the moment a question moves from preparation into response.

## Companion Implementation Commitment

Every companion component will be judged against one question: **Does this feel like an evidence-led rehearsal room, or a generic interview-question generator?**
